# 50 — 2000 Web · DESIGO® build plan

> **Priority style (client request, 2026-10-03)**

**Fit score: 1.5 / 5 for the main site** · **Best used for:** a time-boxed campaign microsite such as
`/2003` ("If DESIGO® had a website in 2003"), built around the returnable glass loop and doorstep delivery, an
idea older than the internet, plus a set of social posts and an April-1 easter egg. It is never the main site.

---

## 1. Style essence

"2000 web" is the vernacular of the early-2000s internet itself: table layouts, 11 px Verdana, bevelled grey
buttons, gradient title bars borrowed from Windows XP, tiled backgrounds, scrolling marquees, hit counters,
"under construction" signs, guestbooks, web rings, "best viewed at 1024×768" footers and Flash intros with a "skip
intro" link. It is nostalgic and handmade, a web made by individuals before platforms smoothed everything.

Three reference points:
1. **GeoCities and Angelfire home pages** (preserved by the Internet Archive and the GeoCities "One Terabyte of
   Kilobyte Age" project): guestbooks, counters, animated GIFs.
2. **Windows XP "Luna" and Mac OS 9 chrome**: bevelled buttons, gradient title bars, 3D inset fields.
3. **Early Indian e-commerce and portals** (Rediff, Indiatimes and early railway booking pages): dense tables,
   coloured header bars and a navigation column on the left. These are the local memory for Indian visitors.

### How it differs from 17 Y2K
| | 17 Y2K | 50 2000 Web |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Fashion, product and music design of 1998–2004 | The *web interface* of 1999–2005 |
| Signature | Chrome, translucent plastic, iridescence, blobs | Bevels, tables, Verdana, counters, title bars, tiled backgrounds |
| Feeling | Futuristic optimism | Homemade, earnest and a little clumsy |
| DESIGO® use | Youth campaign about glass | Campaign about doorstep delivery and an "old idea, new identity" |

## 2. Why it fits DESIGO® and where it fights

- **The story has a genuine nostalgia hook.** Glass bottles on the doorstep are an old idea; DESIGO® gives each
  bottle a QR identity. "The milkman, now with a URL" is a campaign that a 2003 web page tells with a wink.
- **It is honest by default.** The style's "under construction" convention is a charming way to show what is
  pending (360 frames, photographs, approvals), which the brand has to show anyway.
- **It is memorable and shareable**, and cheap to make: HTML, CSS and a few pixel assets.

**Where it fights:** it is the opposite of "Apple product launch × luxury editorial". Used on the main site it
would make a premium brand look like a hobby page, cheapen the bottle, and suggest a low-tech traceability system.
Small Verdana text fails accessibility. The remedy: one campaign URL, clearly framed as a playful time capsule, with
an exit link back to the real site on every screen.

### Typography (open-licence stand-ins for the 2003 web-safe fonts)
- **Verdana / Tahoma role:** *DejaVu Sans* (Bitstream Vera licence, free) for body and UI, at **15 px minimum**,
  which is larger than 2003 but readable today.
- **Georgia role:** *Gelasio* (OFL, metric-compatible with Georgia) for headings, bold, sometimes with a 1 px drop shadow.
- **Counters and LED:** *VT323* (OFL).
- **Display exception:** the DESIGO® wordmark stays the real wordmark; it is never rebuilt as WordArt.

### Texture, imagery, iconography
- Tiled backgrounds, 1 px dotted rules, "horizontal rule" dividers drawn in CSS.
- Imagery: product renders shown in bevelled frames with a "wet floor" reflection (a 2004–06 nostalgia touch).
  Photography is shown at small thumbnail size with a "click to enlarge" link, as in 2003.
- Icons: 16×16 and 32×32 pixel icons drawn in-house (bottle, cow line, envelope, folder, globe), never copied from
  Windows icon sets.

### Grid
A centred **780 px "table"** (the 800×600-safe width of the era) on desktop, built with CSS grid: a 160 px left
navigation column, a 460 px content column and a 160 px right column of "boxes". A 2 px bevelled border frames the
whole page. Mobile collapses to one column with the nav as a top bar, because 2003 had no mobile web and there is no
honest way to imitate one.

### Header wordmark
The black write/un-write DESIGO® loop sits on a white "banner" cell at the top of the table, centred, like a 468×60
banner slot, unchanged.

## 4. Motion and interaction language

| Motion | Spec |
|---|---|
| Intro | A 2.4 s "Loading DESIGO®…" progress bar of 12 bevelled blocks (one block per 200 ms, `steps(12)`), with **SKIP INTRO** visible from the first frame |
| Marquee | A CSS-animated news ticker at 40 px/s with a pause button and pause-on-hover; never the `<marquee>` element |
| Button press | Bevel inverts (light ↔ dark edges) and the label shifts 1 px down-right, 0 ms (instant, as in 2003) |
| Page change | No transition; a 120 ms fake "loading" hourglass cursor, then the next page |
| GIF-style animation | Frame-stepped loops at 8–12 fps using `steps()`; at most 2 per page |
| Hit counter | Digits roll once on load (600 ms, `steps(10)`) |

**Cursor states:** default is the classic arrow (a custom 32 px pixel arrow in charcoal) · **link** is the pixel
pointing hand · **busy** is a pixel hourglass, shown only for the 120 ms fake load · **360** is a pixel "↔" hand ·
**text** is the I-beam · **disabled** is the arrow with a small "no" circle. On touch, the default system behaviour
is kept.

**Hover:** links turn from underlined green to underlined earth with a highlight background (`#F8E4C2`); buttons
get a 1 px dotted focus rectangle, the authentic Windows focus indicator, kept as the visible focus style.

## 5. The hero bottle and the four variants

The bottle appears in a bevelled "window" titled `bottle.jpg`, standing on a wet-floor reflection (a flipped render
at 18% opacity fading to 0 over 40% of its height). Float is replaced by stillness: in 2003 nothing floated.

- **Before 360 frames:** the window shows the static render with a small caption "360° view: under construction"
  and the classic striped tape graphic. This is the honest moment of the whole style.
- **After 360 frames:** the frames are down-sampled to **24 frames at 12 fps** and play as a "rotating 3D bottle GIF"
  (an image sprite stepped with CSS). Clicking "View in 3D!" opens a pop-up-style window (a modal, not a real
  pop-up) containing the full Bottle360Viewer with drag; the counter reads `Frame 36 of 72` in DejaVu Sans.

| Variant | 2000-web world |
|---|---|
| **MASTER 26** | A forest-skinned window with a tiled leaf pattern background; the "Product of the Month!" box (it is a 2003 convention, not a claim). Line: "Twenty-six herbs. The fullest expression of the source." with herb count pending. |
| **ROOT 14** | Red skin; the bottle on a small red-earth photo strip; "Fourteen herbs, rooted in free grazing." (pending). |
| **BASE 3** | Amber skin; a "sunset.jpg" banner (C3 at thumbnail size); "The everyday foundation." |

**Product info box:** a bevelled two-column table: `Code: DESIGO® V1+` · `Price:` with a dotted underline and

## 10. Performance, accessibility and mobile

- Tiny by design: no frameworks beyond the site's own, sprites under 400 KB, fonts subset.
- Accessibility: real semantic HTML (CSS grid, not layout tables); body ≥ 15 px; nothing blinks or flashes; the
  marquee and GIF loops pause and stop in reduced motion; the dotted Windows focus rectangle doubles as a strong
  visible focus style; pixel cursors fall back to system cursors at high contrast.
- Mobile: one column, top nav, the bottle window full-width; the intro is skipped on mobile entirely.
- Guestbook: server-side moderation, rate limiting and no public email addresses.

## 11. Risks and premium guardrails

**Risks:** cheapening the brand; implying the traceability system is old-fashioned; inaccessible small type;
irony that excludes older visitors; spam in the guestbook.

**Premium guardrails**
1. Campaign URL only, with a start and end date and a permanent exit link to the real site.
2. The product render, the wordmark and the reserve flow are never degraded: the bottle stays high resolution
   inside its bevelled window.
3. Every counter is real or labelled `DEMO`. Nothing invents visitors, bottles or farms.
4. No blinking text, no auto-playing sound, no real pop-ups.
5. Copy is affectionate, not mocking: the joke is on the 2003 web, never on milkmen, farmers or cows.
6. No health or "best" claims, even as period-style jokes ("World's best milk!!" is exactly what 2003 pages said and
   exactly what DESIGO® must never say).
7. The traceability demo is clearly labelled as a playful demo and links to the real /trace.
